# User Needs & Sentiment: Digital D&D 5e Companion Tools (as of Nov 2026)

Evidence quality legend: **[Q]** = quantitative (company figure / poll / survey), **[A]** = anecdote (forum/Reddit comment, single review), **[S]** = secondary aggregator of uncertain methodology. Dates flagged where older than 2024.

## 1. Surveys and data on tool usage

### Takeaway
Hard, current usage data is thin. The strongest figures are WotC/Hasbro statements about D&D Beyond (DDB): about 30M registered accounts, and a disputed claim that "3 in 4" D&D players use it (July 2026). The Roll20 Orr Report stopped in 2021–22. VTT share data comes only from self-selected forum polls. DDB is the default tool, so dnd-sistem is positioned as an alternative to it, not as a new category.

### Cited Findings
- [Q] An EN World thread (Jul 29, 2026) reports that a Hasbro financial call claimed "3 out of 4 D&D players" use DDB and that DDB has 30 million registered accounts. Commenters questioned the method: registered accounts are not active users, and the claim may have walked back an earlier "3 in 4 TTRPG players reached" framing. — [EN World](https://www.enworld.org/posts/9954744/)
- [Q] Earlier DDB figures: about 10M users at the April 2022 acquisition and 18M+ registered by Sept 2024 (WotC, via Gaming Trend). About 20.6M in mid-2025 comes from community tracking and is not official. — [Voxbooster stats roundup (S)](https://voxbooster.com/blog/dnd-ttrpg-statistics-2026)
- [Q] The Roll20 Orr Group Industry Report ran quarterly from 2014 to 2021 and was confirmed "on hold" in 2022. Its last figures are from 2020–21: D&D was about 60% of Roll20 games, play hours exceeded 100M per quarter, and Roll20 had about 10M accounts (2022). All of this is **dated and pre-2024**. — [Roll20 wiki](https://wiki.roll20.net/Orr_Report); [Orr Q4 2021](https://blog.roll20.net/posts/the-orr-report-q4-2021); [Voxbooster (S)](https://voxbooster.com/blog/dnd-ttrpg-statistics-2026)
- [Q, self-selected] An EN World VTT poll (March 2025) put Foundry at 38.9%, Roll20 at 21.7% and Owlbear Rodeo at 12.8%. Another EN World poll had Foundry at 41.8% and Roll20 at 36.3%. A commenter noted that no VTT publishes comparable numbers ("we really have no idea who is the largest"). — [EN World poll thread](https://www.enworld.org/goto/post?id=9602614)
- [Q] Compare The Market surveyed about 3,000 people in the US, Canada and Australia (Jan 2025). About 13% of US respondents say they collect or play TTRPGs or wargames. The survey did not measure VTT use or in-person vs online play. — [EN World](https://www.enworld.org/threads/compare-the-markets-tabletop-gaming-survey.712719/)
- [S, conflicting] Aggregator sites give in-person vs online splits that cannot be reconciled: "76% prefer in-person," "78% in-person / 22% remote (2023)," "35% in-person / 40% online / 25% mix," and VTT use anywhere from 48% to 89%. None traces to a verifiable primary source. — [RollHoard](https://rollhoard.com/guides/tabletop-rpg-statistics-2026/); [WorldMetrics](https://worldmetrics.org/ttrpg-industry-statistics/); [ZipDo](https://zipdo.co/ttrpg-industry-statistics/)
- [Q] WotC's own digital bets have been mixed. About 90% of the Sigil 3D VTT team was laid off in March 2025 because its "distinct monetization path will not be realized." DDB announced Sigil's shutdown in Oct 2025, while the 2D Maps VTT stays on DDB. — [PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/games/hasbro-pushed-sigil-out-of-the-nest-d-and-ds-latest-layoffs-happened-because-the-distinct-monetization-path-for-its-virtual-tabletop-sigil-never-materialized); [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/gaming/hasbro-laid-off-the-team-behind-its-virtual-tabletop-app-only-weeks-after-it-was-released-214024876.html); [ComicBook](https://comicbook.com/gaming/news/dnd-is-shutting-down-its-failed-vtt-experiment-and-fans-know-exactly-what-should-come-next/)
- [Q, platform rating] Google Play shows DDB at about 4.6/5 from about 113k reviews (via an aggregator). Trustpilot gives DDB the web service 2/5 from 55 reviews. App-store ratings are far kinder than web-service reviews, and Trustpilot complaints centre on support, billing and account lockouts. — [AppFollow](https://apps.appfollow.io/ios/d-d-beyond/1263629972?country=kr); [Trustpilot](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/www.dndbeyond.com)

### Inferences
- DDB is the dominant default for 5e character sheets, so most target users already have a DDB habit. dnd-sistem should compete on the things DDB does poorly (see sections 2–3), not on content breadth.
- Online-play tooling is fragmented across Foundry, Roll20 and Owlbear, and WotC's own 3D VTT failed. A solo developer should not chase the VTT space. The companion and at-table niche is where the needs below go unmet.

### Gaps
- No credible 2024–2026 survey of digital tool use at the in-person table. The Quinns survey and any 2025 "Orr-like" report could not be located.
- No DDB monthly-active-user number. No public Foundry, Owlbear or Demiplane user counts. No Kickstarter or itch data was found for companion apps.
- The DDB App Store review page failed to load, so iOS review themes come from forum and aggregator data only.

## 2. Most requested or praised features

### Takeaway
Across 2025–26 threads, players praise four things: automation (auto-calculated bonuses, spell DCs, condition and exhaustion trackers), searchable reference, custom or homebrew content that feeds into the sheet, and simplicity with zero cost. The demand for a shared homebrew repository that the DM pushes to players is **explicitly unmet**. Paper sheets plus a phone for spells is the common in-person pattern.

### Cited Findings
- [A] In a popular "alternatives to DDB" thread (r/dndnext, about June 2025), a free Google Sheets template with separate 2014 and 2024 versions got 43 points. It was praised for automating stats, attack and skill bonuses and spell save DCs, and for its condition and exhaustion trackers. Commenters also praised Roll20's free sheets ("avoid buying digital content they already own") and the official fillable PDF for its simplicity. Dicecloud was called "overly complicated but good once learned," and Foundry has "a learning curve." — [Reddit snapshot](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1r8jvv3/snapshots/2026-02-23T09%3A34%3A42.266568Z)
- [A] In a homebrew-sheet thread (r/dndnext, about June 2025), a DM with third-party classes and about 200 new spells wanted players to pull from one shared repository at an in-person table. **No commenter knew a tool that does this.** One Roll20 user hand-built each sheet at about 30 minutes apiece, another suggested a shared Google Drive folder, and DDB was praised for custom spells and items that feed bonuses into sheets (paid subscription). — [Reddit snapshot](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1ra0k1r/snapshots/2026-02-23T09%3A34%3A42.266568Z)
- [A] In an r/rpg tablet-at-table thread (about Oct 2025), commenters valued fast PDF and rules search, automatic stat updates, searchable notes, and resource tracking during constant HP and MP swings. Pathbuilder, D&D Beyond, Obsidian and OneNote were named. — [Reddit snapshot](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1sqozaq/snapshots/2026-04-20T19%3A33%3A50.512576Z)
- [A] On combat and initiative trackers for in-person play, an App Store listing for Simple Initiative (2025–26) advertises "no ads, no subscriptions, no account" and auto-sorted initiative. That pitch signals what the market wants. Many DMs still prefer paper trackers with columns for HP, AC and conditions. The DDB Combat Tracker is limited to Hero or Master tier subscribers. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6761733442); [Arcane Eye](https://arcaneeye.com/dm-tools-5e/best-dnd-5e-initiative-tracker/)
- [A] DM visibility of player sheets is a stated reason groups adopt digital sheets. One group "entirely abandoned paper" because DDB sheets were better "for both players and DMs." Others keep an exported or printed PDF as a backup. — [Search summary of Reddit/EN World threads](https://www.enworld.org/goto/post?id=9827404)
- [A] A beginner-friendly creation flow is wanted. DDB shipped a Quickbuilder that one forum user found "clear and accessible" for reaching level 1 quickly, while others said it "never had to make real choices." — [DDB forum](https://www.dndbeyond.com/forums/d-d-beyond-general/general-discussion/237910-new-quick-builder); [DDB post](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/2135-behind-the-screen-the-new-quickbuilder-and-future?page=4)
- [A] Offline access: DDB still lacks a full offline mode, according to a July 2025 explainer (secondary). Older forum threads say only fully purchased compendiums download, and that shared-campaign content is unreadable in the app once the sharer's subscription lapses. A staff reply called offline "on the roadmap" (undated). — [DDB forum (pricing FAQ)](https://www.dndbeyond.com/forums/d-d-beyond-general/d-d-beyond-feedback/5743-d-d-beyond-pricing-purchase-faq-discussion?page=25); [DDB mobile feedback](https://www.dndbeyond.com/forums/d-d-beyond-mobile-app-feedback/193640-d-d-beyond-app-on-ios/next)

### Inferences (ranked demand signal for an at-table companion)
1. **Automated math plus resource, condition and concentration tracking.** This is the most consistently praised value of a digital sheet over paper and the main reason people put up with apps.
2. **Free, no account friction, no paywall on core functions.** Free and no-account tools are explicitly what people recommend as DDB alternatives.
3. **Clean 2014 vs 2024 separation.** People want explicit edition choice per character (see section 3).
4. **DM view of player sheets plus a shared homebrew pool pushed to players.** This is a stated, unmet need and a real gap for dnd-sistem's campaign feature.
5. **Fast search and reference on a phone.** DDB's mobile app is called "very time consuming" to navigate.
6. **Offline reliability at the table**, plus PDF or print export as a backup.
7. **Simple combat or initiative tracker** with HP, AC and conditions, which DDB paywalls.
- Dice with modifiers is wanted but polarising (see section 4). It should be optional, not central.

### Gaps
- No quantitative ranking of feature requests was found. The ordering above is inferred from frequency and upvotes in threads.
- No direct data on demand for automated short and long rests specifically. It falls under resource tracking.

## 3. Common complaints

### Takeaway
The dominant complaints are about money and trust: subscriptions, content sharing tied to a paid tier, buying content twice for physical and digital, and paywalled DM tools. Next come 2014/2024 rules contamination on DDB, mobile UX friction, and complexity in power tools (Foundry, Dicecloud). AI backlash is strong among gamers generally, but TTRPG-specific quantitative data is lacking.

### Cited Findings
- [A] "Don't want to support DND Beyond" thread (June 2025): the poster objects to DDB's "new pricing model." Replies point to free options (Roll20 sheets, Google Sheets, fillable PDF, Owlbear) and to ethical avoidance of WotC-licensed tools. — [Reddit snapshot](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1r8jvv3/snapshots/2026-02-23T09%3A34%3A42.266568Z)
- [A] A critique of DDB "Drops" (digital-exclusive content) says the traditional reason to subscribe was content sharing, and that every player now has to pay at least $25.99/yr to access certain spells or feats. The source is undated, likely 2024–25. — [EN World](https://www.enworld.org/threads/d-d-beyond-drops-are-antithetical-to-d-d-beyonds-traditional-subscription-model.719049/page-26)
- [A] A marketplace redesign removed cheap à-la-carte purchases; previously a subclass cost about $3. Users ran a "bring back à la carte" thread. — [DungeonsAndDragonsFan](https://dungeonsanddragonsfan.com/dnd-beyond-marketplace-redesign); [DDB forum](https://www.dndbeyond.com/forums/d-d-beyond-general/d-d-beyond-feedback/197218-bring-back-a-la-carte?page=7)
- [Q-ish, 2023, dated] During the OGL crisis, so many users cancelled DDB subscriptions that the cancellation page crashed. WotC denied rumours of a $30 tier. This shows the trust issue runs deep. — [GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/so-many-fans-cancel-their-online-dandd-subscriptions-that-it-crashes-the-page-following-backlash); [GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/dandd-calls-rumors-of-dollar30-subs-ai-dms-and-ignored-feedback-false)
- [A] Buying twice and lock-in: forum users say there is "no way to justify paying the full RL purchase price and a subscription" without offline access, and Roll20 free sheets were praised for avoiding re-buying owned books. — [DDB forum](https://www.dndbeyond.com/forums/d-d-beyond-general/d-d-beyond-feedback/5743-d-d-beyond-pricing-purchase-faq-discussion?page=25); [Reddit snapshot](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1r8jvv3/snapshots/2026-02-23T09%3A34%3A42.266568Z)
- [Q-ish event] On 2014/2024 mixing, DDB planned in Aug 2024 to auto-update 2014 sheets to 2024 rules and reversed after backlash. Bug threads since then report 2014 sheets showing 2024 mechanics, such as a 2014 monk showing the 2024 1d8 Martial Arts die and 2024 Grapple and Shove. Users say "no way to make a fully 2014 character sheet." Roll20 markets support for both 2014 and 2024 spells. — [Escapist](https://escapistmagazine.com/dd-beyond-pledges-to-retain-2014-character-sheets-after-backlash); [DDB bug thread](https://www.dndbeyond.com/forums/d-d-beyond-general/bugs-support/208156-bug-in-all-2014-character-sheets-displays?comment=1); [ComicBook](https://comicbook.com/gaming/news/dungeons-dragons-dndbeyond-spells-changes-character-sheet-2024)
- [A] On mobile performance and UX, finding info in the DDB phone app is "very time consuming," and "digital on a phone is a bad experience." — [Search summary of Reddit threads](https://redlib.nohost.network/r/dndnext/comments/1f9doc5/irl_replacement_for_dnd_beyond_character_sheets/llmqt7g) (page 403'd; quotes from search snippet)
- [A] Trustpilot and aggregators report unresponsive support, unexpected charges and account lockouts at DDB. — [Trustpilot](https://ca.trustpilot.com/review/www.dndbeyond.com)
- [Q, video games not TTRPG] Quantic Foundry (late 2025) found 85% of gamers below neutral on gen-AI in games and 63% at the most negative option. AI-generated quests and dialogue are now 77–83% negative, up from 46% in 2024. This is a proxy for DM-content AI features. A TTRPG-specific ACM study (Oct 2025) is qualitative only. — [GamesMarket](https://www.gamesmarket.global/quantic-foundry-most-gamers-are-negative-about-the-use-of-gen-ai-in-video-games); [BoingBoing](https://boingboing.net/2025/12/20/survey-finds-very-negative-attitude-toward-gen-ai-in-games.html); [ACM DOI](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1145%2F3748630)
- [A] Rumours of "AI DMs" at DDB were publicly denied by WotC, which shows how sensitive the community is to the topic. — [GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/dandd-calls-rumors-of-dollar30-subs-ai-dms-and-ignored-feedback-false)

### Inferences
- "Free, no paywall, your data is yours (export)" is a credible differentiator. Trust is the wedge, not features.
- Strict per-character edition tagging (2014 vs 2024) with no leakage is a concrete quality bar that DDB visibly fails.
- Avoid shipping or marketing generative-AI features. Backlash risk outweighs the upside for this audience.

### Gaps
- No 2025–26 quantitative data on DDB churn or subscription numbers.
- The specific 2025 DDB "pricing model change" referenced by the Reddit OP could not be pinned down. It is possibly Drops or the marketplace redesign.

## 4. In-person (phone at table) vs online play needs

### Takeaway
Online players get sheets bundled into their VTT (Foundry, Roll20) and value integration. In-person players want a fast, glanceable, low-distraction phone companion. Many use a hybrid of paper sheet plus phone for spells, and a vocal segment rejects screens entirely. Reliability is decisive in person ("I don't want a technical glitch gumming up combat").

### Cited Findings
- [A] In the r/rpg "Do you use a tablet in pen-and-paper RPGs?" thread (about Oct 2025), the top comment (36 points) was "If I'm playing in person I'm 100% analog." A close second (34 points) said mixing is fine. Other comments: a paper sheet with DDB on the phone for spells; a GM who avoids digital in combat for fear of glitches; digital dice feel "less random" and "slightly lame"; annoyance at players checking phones; screen fatigue. — [Reddit snapshot](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1sqozaq/snapshots/2026-04-20T19%3A33%3A50.512576Z)
- [A] In online play, the top-voted advice (95 points) is that "most VTTs include character sheets," so online users rarely need a standalone sheet. — [Reddit snapshot](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1r8jvv3/snapshots/2026-02-23T09%3A34%3A42.266568Z)
- [A] Roll20 ships a mobile D&D sheet, but users note "it's still using your phone's browser." Some print Roll20 sheets for in-person games. — [Roll20 blog](https://blog.roll20.net/posts/dd-sheet-now-on-phones); [Roll20 forum](https://app.roll20.net/forum/permalink/12690749)
- [A] At the in-person table, DMs commonly use a laptop for notes and stat blocks and a paper or iPad initiative tracker. — [Arcane Eye](https://arcaneeye.com/dm-tools-5e/best-dnd-5e-initiative-tracker/)

### Inferences
- For dnd-sistem (mobile-first web): prioritise offline/PWA reliability, a big-tap "play mode" (HP, slots, conditions, concentration on one screen), and minimal navigation depth. Physical-dice users need a way to enter a rolled result, and in-app dice should be optional.
- Online-play features (maps, VTT) are low priority because users already get sheets inside their VTT.

### Gaps
- No reliable quantitative in-person vs online split for 2024–26 (the aggregator figures conflict).

## 5. New-player onboarding pain points

### Takeaway
New players are overwhelmed by the volume of options in full builders. Quick or premade builders help them get to the table but are criticised for skipping learning. The ideal is guided choices with plain-language explanations. Evidence is anecdotal.

### Cited Findings
- [A] DDB forum thread: "Overwhelmed, effectively new player." — [DDB forum](https://www.dndbeyond.com/forums/d-d-beyond-general/general-discussion/65480-overwhelmed-effectively-new-player) (likely pre-2024)
- [A] DDB's own guidance says the Standard builder is best for players "comfortable navigating all of the options" and recommends premades for newcomers. — [DDB post](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/1059-how-to-create-your-first-dungeons-dragons)
- [A] Quickbuilder feedback: "clear and accessible… gets a new player to a level 1 character quickly" vs "never had to make real choices" and "feels like picking from a list of premade characters." — [DDB forum](https://www.dndbeyond.com/forums/d-d-beyond-general/general-discussion/237910-new-quick-builder); [DDB post comments](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/2135-behind-the-screen-the-new-quickbuilder-and-future?page=4)
- [A] PC Gamer covered DDB's "slick new online character creator" that makes a hero "in seconds," which shows WotC sees onboarding speed as a priority. — [PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/d-and-d-beyonds-slick-new-online-character-creator-lets-you-make-a-hero-in-seconds)
- [A] Tools with power-user depth (Dicecloud, Foundry) are repeatedly described as having a learning curve or being "overly complicated." — [Reddit snapshot](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1r8jvv3/snapshots/2026-02-23T09%3A34%3A42.266568Z)

### Inferences
- Opportunity: a guided level-1 builder with recommended defaults, one-line explanations of each choice, and later "what does this do?" tooltips on the sheet. A DM-invite link that drops a new player straight into a campaign with allowed options pre-filtered would reduce friction further.

### Gaps
- No quantitative data on drop-off during character creation. No 2025–26 survey on new-player tool experience.
