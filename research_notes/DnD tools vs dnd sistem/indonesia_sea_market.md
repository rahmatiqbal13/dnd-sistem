# Indonesian and SEA D&D 5e Market and Digital Tool Usage (as of November 2026)

Research date: 2026-11-04. About 15 searches and fetches. Most community evidence comes from one detailed insider blog post (Jan 2026) and older press (2019–2022). Hard numbers are scarce. Every item below is marked as evidence, inference or gap.

## 1. Size and character of the Indonesian D&D community

### Takeaway
The Indonesian D&D scene is small, Jakarta-centred and Discord-first. It has two anchor Discord servers, D&D Indonesia and DnD Jakarta (both formed around 2017), a few city servers and a growing grassroots convention circuit (DEX, LFGcon). Interest came in waves: Critical Role (2016), COVID (2020–21), Raditya Dika's videos and Baldur's Gate 3 (2023–25). The most-cited structural problem is "many players, few DMs".

### Cited Findings
- The first big Indonesian Discord communities, D&D Indonesia (focused on 5e) and D&D Jakarta, both formed in 2017. Notable local variants include D&D Yogyakarta, Rigor Mortis (a smaller server open to non-D&D games, which spun off the DEX community) and ID:TRPG (began as a podcast and is not D&D-focused). Post dated 29 Jan 2026. — [Afraid of Encounters: Indonesian TTRPG scene & LFG](https://afraidofencounters.bearblog.dev/afraid-of-communities-indonesian-tabletop-rpg-scene-lfg/)
- Timeline of growth waves from the same post: before 2014 the scene was small groups of high-school and college students, with early-2000s players on AD&D 2e, 3.0 and 3.5. Then came a 2016 Critical Role wave, Discord growth in 2017 and again during COVID-19, and a 2023–2025 "Radit Wave" (Raditya Dika) plus a "Baldur's Gate 3 Wave". An unnamed large Indonesian Discord saw a surge of members after Raditya Dika's video linked to it. — [Afraid of Encounters](https://afraidofencounters.bearblog.dev/afraid-of-communities-indonesian-tabletop-rpg-scene-lfg/)
- The blog says the scene has many players but few Dungeon Masters (as summarised by search results), and that English rulebooks were a barrier for new players. — [Afraid of Encounters](https://afraidofencounters.bearblog.dev/afraid-of-communities-indonesian-tabletop-rpg-scene-lfg/)
- Discord member count: a third-party listing shows the D&D Indonesia server (ID 360721640558166016) at about 7,830 members, about 2,243 online. This is an undated snapshot. — [Discord Bot List: D&D Indonesia](https://discordbotlist.com/servers/dungeons-dragons-indonesia-360721640558166016); official listing at [discord.com/servers/d-d-indonesia-360721640558166016](https://discord.com/servers/d-d-indonesia-360721640558166016). The server describes itself as an Indonesia-based TTRPG server where people can host, play or watch games.
- A Reddit r/bali thread (2025) points newcomers to D&D Indonesia, which has a Bali regional group, and to "DNDJKT", described as "more populated". The poster said they could not post on r/indonesia because of karma limits. — [r/bali thread mirror](https://pol1.lr.ggtyler.dev/r/bali/comments/1jase0t/is_dungeons_and_dragons_the_ttrpg_game_something). The fetch failed and the content comes from search snippets.
- The Jakarta Post (Feb 2022) says Indonesian D&D communities "grow online". DnD Jakarta (founder Indra Aziz, playing since 2001) moved from in-person play to its Discord server during COVID. One player featured in the article played Curse of Strahd with five friends over the DnD Jakarta Discord. The article says younger Indonesians often first heard of D&D through TV shows. Player Afiat Yudhistira is quoted naming D&D Indonesia and DnD Jakarta Discord as the largest communities. The article is paywalled, so details come partly from snippets. — [The Jakarta Post, 18 Feb 2022](https://www.thejakartapost.com/culture/2022/02/18/indonesian-dungeons-dragons-communities-grow-online.html)
- The Jakarta Post (Dec 2019): "As cool as e-sports: tabletop games gain popularity in Jakarta" (older context). — [Jakarta Post 2019](https://www.thejakartapost.com/news/2019/12/11/as-cool-as-e-sports-tabletop-games-gain-popularity-in-jakarta)
- Venues:
  - Fantasy 'n Games (South Jakarta) is possibly the earliest store selling D&D products and hosting sessions.
  - Arcanum (Kuningan City) is "probably the largest" in Jakarta.
  - Castle 8 is in Depok.
  - Source: [Afraid of Encounters](https://afraidofencounters.bearblog.dev/afraid-of-communities-indonesian-tabletop-rpg-scene-lfg/)
  - Grouplay / Mamasays (Gandaria City) runs weekly events and has "an active Dungeons & Dragons (DnD) community". — [NOW! Jakarta board game cafes](https://www.nowjakarta.co.id/?p=103470)
  - Good Guy Games (Tomang) runs weekly tournaments with games including D&D. — [What's New Indonesia: board game stores Jakarta](https://whatsnewindonesia.com/ultimate-guide/jakarta/shops/best-board-game-stores-jakarta)
  - Arcanum regularly hosts card-game gatherings and posts its schedules in DnD Jakarta. — [Boardoro: Arcanum Hobbies](https://boardoro.com/en/store/arcanum-hobbies-board-games-playground)
  - West Jakarta has the most board game cafés in Indonesia. — [GoodStats infographic](https://goodstats.id/infographic/jakbar-jadi-wilayah-dengan-board-game-cafe-terbanyak-di-indonesia-IBE0E)
  - Board-game cafés are described as a trendy "third space" (Mar 2025). — [Jakarta Post Weekender](https://weekender.thejakartapost.com/prosperity-and-progress/2025/03/28/trendy-tabletop-board-game-cafs-are-your-next-favorite-third-space.html)
- Conventions and events:
  - DEX (Dungeon Explorers) was the first Indonesian TTRPG event unrelated to board or card games. Its Oct 2019 event had 40+ participants, and its "D&D Epic" is a multi-table session.
  - LFGcon SESSION 0 (1 Nov 2025) had 18 participants.
  - LFGcon SESSION 1 (24 Jan 2026, Central Jakarta) had 40+ participants, and registration sold out within one hour. Future LFGcons were planned roughly quarterly.
  - Jakarta Tabletop Day focuses mainly on board and card games.
  - TPK (The Pandemonium Kollektive) is a community started by paid GMs.
  - Source: [Afraid of Encounters](https://afraidofencounters.bearblog.dev/afraid-of-communities-indonesian-tabletop-rpg-scene-lfg/)
- Raditya Dika, one of Indonesia's largest YouTubers, made D&D videos referenced in the scene blog, which ties them to the 2023–25 "Radit Wave". A web search found no standalone press coverage, series title or view counts. — [Afraid of Encounters](https://afraidofencounters.bearblog.dev/afraid-of-communities-indonesian-tabletop-rpg-scene-lfg/); [Raditya Dika on Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raditya_Dika)

### Inferences
- An order-of-magnitude estimate, not evidence: the engaged online community is in the low tens of thousands at most. The largest server is about 8k members, servers overlap, and convention turnout is in the tens. There is a much larger "aware" audience from the Raditya Dika and BG3 waves that has never played.
- "Many players, few DMs" plus "English rulebooks are a barrier" are the two pains that most directly suggest an Indonesian-language companion. It could lower DM prep load and help onboard newcomers who arrive from YouTube or BG3.
- Discord is the distribution channel. A tool that shares easily into Discord (links, embeds, a bot) fits existing behaviour.
- Bandung and Surabaya: no evidence found (see gaps). Jakarta, Depok, Yogyakarta and Bali are the evidenced hubs.

### Gaps
- Current (Nov 2026) verified member counts for DnD Jakarta, D&D Indonesia and the city servers. The Discord Bot List figure is undated.
- Facebook group sizes, and Instagram or TikTok creator lists other than Raditya Dika. Search gave no reliable results.
- Raditya Dika's D&D video titles, dates and view counts.
- Any survey of Indonesian player demographics (age, gender, student vs working) or in-person vs online split.
- Communities and stores in Bandung and Surabaya.
- Whether LFGcon actually held its planned April–June 2026 event or later ones.

## 2. Indonesian-language D&D tools, translations and local TTRPG systems

### Takeaway
No Indonesian translation of the D&D 5e SRD, official or fan, and no Indonesian-language 5e glossary or tool turned up in searches. Wizards of the Coast's official languages do not include Indonesian. Local creative output exists but is small, mostly in English, and indie and OSR-leaning.

### Cited Findings
- Searching in Indonesian for SRD and glossary translations returned nothing Indonesian. It did turn up official or fan translations in other languages: the official French SRD (CC, Jan 2023), German SRD5.1 sites and a Spanish SRD 2024 Foundry module. — [dnddeutsch.de SRD](https://www.dnddeutsch.de/srd); [Foundry translate-dnd5e-sdr2-es](https://foundryvtt.com/packages/translate-dnd5e-sdr2-es)
- Wizards says it will focus on English, French, German, Italian, Japanese and Spanish. SRD 5.2.1 is available in French, Italian, German and Spanish, but not Portuguese or Indonesian. — [D&D Beyond: change to supported languages](https://www.dndbeyond.com/posts/1669-change-to-supported-languages-in-d-d-product); [D&D Beyond support: 2024 core rulebooks in other languages](https://dndbeyond-support.wizards.com/hc/en-us/articles/46247021698452-Will-the-2024-Core-Rulebooks-be-available-in-other-supported-languages)
- Local systems and publishers named in the scene blog:
  - Kutuk, an Indonesian horror TTRPG released in 2022 on DriveThruRPG.
  - OnMeja Games (Desyanto), which publishes Quack Healer and Wanaloka and the DURF adventure The Jewel of Sangwija, and started with board games.
  - The blog mentions no translations.
  - A separate web search could not independently confirm Kutuk or Wanaloka listings.
  - Source: [Afraid of Encounters](https://afraidofencounters.bearblog.dev/afraid-of-communities-indonesian-tabletop-rpg-scene-lfg/)
- The Indonesian TTRPG Blogwagon (started 17 Aug, Independence Day) collected 22 posts, 18 by Indonesians, of playable Indonesian-setting content. Examples include a Liminal Horror statblock for the Pocong and a magic system based on Indonesian occult practice. — [Blogwagon roundup](https://afraidofencounters.bearblog.dev/indonesian-ttrpg-blogwagon-roundup/); [Blogwagon announcement](https://afraidofencounters.bearblog.dev/indonesian-ttrpg-blogwagon/)
- Indonesian TTRPG items found on itch.io include "Sebuah Cerita" by Hump Games, and Mausantara (BackerKit) references Jakarta OSR sessions. Neither was verified as an Indonesian-language system. — [Sebuah Cerita on itch.io](https://humpgames.itch.io/sebuah-cerita); [Mausantara on BackerKit](https://www.backerkit.com/c/projects/i-m-big/mausantara/updates)

### Inferences
- An Indonesian-language 5e rules and SRD companion appears to fill an empty niche. The absence of evidence is not proof, since Discord-internal glossaries or Google Docs could exist and would not be indexed.
- The SRD 5.1 and 5.2.1 are released under CC-BY-4.0, which legally allows a fan Indonesian translation with attribution. This is from prior knowledge of the licence and should be verified against the SRD text.
- Local creators lean toward non-D&D indie and OSR games and folklore content. An optional Nusantara-flavoured homebrew or bestiary layer could resonate with them, but the core 5e audience is the larger one.

### Gaps
- Any Indonesian fan wiki, glossary (for example agreed Indonesian terms for "saving throw" or "advantage"), or Discord bot localised to Indonesian. None found.
- Whether any Indonesian publisher has licensed D&D. No evidence found.

## 3. Tools Indonesian and SEA players use, and complaints

### Takeaway
The evidenced stack is Discord for community and voice, Roll20 for virtual tables, D&D Beyond for character sheets, and YouTube and streaming for discovery. There are almost no Indonesia-specific published complaints. The pain points around English-only content and USD pricing are inferred from structural data (Section 4) and one blog note on English rulebooks.

### Cited Findings
- Tools in the Indonesian scene: Discord, Facebook groups, Roll20, YouTube and streaming (including vtubers), Instagram, itch.io and DriveThruRPG. Ten Candles was played online using candle PNGs. Some actual-play productions use generative AI. — [Afraid of Encounters](https://afraidofencounters.bearblog.dev/afraid-of-communities-indonesian-tabletop-rpg-scene-lfg/)
- DnD Jakarta's sessions moved to Discord during COVID. — [The Jakarta Post 2022](https://www.thejakartapost.com/culture/2022/02/18/indonesian-dungeons-dragons-communities-grow-online.html)
- Philippines: Facebook groups with "thousands of members" (for example D&D Adventurers League Philippines), Tabletop Philippines streams and Roll20 for virtual play. Recruitment ads list "Online (D&D Beyond and Discord)". — [The Red Circle PH: How to play D&D in the Philippines](https://theredcircle.com.ph/?p=3548); [The LaSallian 2020](https://thelasallian.com/2020/01/20/exploring-dd-in-the-philippines/)
- A Reddit comment (second-hand) says Roll20 has no one-off purchase option and locks many features behind a subscription. — Search snippet from the [r/bali thread](https://pol1.lr.ggtyler.dev/r/bali/comments/1jase0t/is_dungeons_and_dragons_the_ttrpg_game_something)
- A D&D Beyond forum thread "can't make purchases" exists, but it is not Indonesia-specific. — [D&D Beyond forums](https://www.dndbeyond.com/forums/d-d-beyond-general/bugs-support/240538-cant-make-purchases)

### Inferences
- Main inferred complaints:
  - English-only UI and rules text.
  - USD pricing through card-based checkout. D&D Beyond and Roll20 sell in USD and mainly accept cards or PayPal; this is prior knowledge and should be checked against current checkout pages.
  - Desktop-centric VTTs on mobile-heavy users (Section 4).
- A free, mobile-first, Indonesian-language web app removes all three barriers at once. The positioning claim would be "no card, no English, no laptop needed".

### Gaps
- No Indonesian-language forum or Reddit posts were found that directly complain about D&D Beyond or Roll20 pricing or card requirements.
- No usage share data (Owlbear, Foundry, Avrae or other Discord bots) for Indonesia or SEA.
- Whether D&D Beyond offers regional pricing or local payment methods in Indonesia was not verified.

## 4. Mobile vs desktop, connectivity and payment methods (monetisation)

### Takeaway
Indonesia is a huge, mobile-first market: 230M internet users and 331M mobile connections as of late 2025. Mobile download speeds now exceed fixed. Credit card ownership is tiny (about 2% of adults, 2021), and payments run on e-wallets and QRIS. Monetisation should use Saweria or Trakteer with QRIS, GoPay, OVO and DANA, not Stripe, card or PayPal-only options.

### Cited Findings
- DataReportal Digital 2026 Indonesia (data from Oct to late 2025, published 5 Nov 2025):

  | Metric | Value |
  |---|---|
  | Internet users | 230M (80.5% of population) |
  | Social media user identities | 180M (62.9%) |
  | Cellular mobile connections | 331M (116%) |
  | Median mobile download speed | 45.01 Mbps |
  | Median fixed download speed | 39.88 Mbps |
  | YouTube ad reach | 151M |
  | Instagram ad reach | 108M |
  | TikTok ad reach (18+) | 180M |

  The report does not give a mobile vs desktop web-traffic split or Discord reach. — [DataReportal Digital 2026: Indonesia](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-indonesia)
- Earlier internet user figures: 212M (Jan 2025) and 185.3M (Jan 2024). — [Digital 2025: Indonesia](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2025-indonesia); [Digital 2024: Indonesia](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2024-indonesia)
- About 2% of Indonesians aged 15+ held a credit card in 2021 (World Bank Global Findex via Statista). The 2024 Findex round exists, but no headline percentage was found. — [Statista: Indonesia credit card holders](https://statista.com/statistics/941999/indonesia-share-credit-card-holders)
- E-wallet share data conflicts because sources measure different things:
  - Jakpat (n=1,945): 72% use DANA. — [GoodStats](https://data.goodstats.id/statistic/5-e-wallet-favorit-publik-ri-dana-di-puncak-LI1QF)
  - Ipsos via 2C2P, e-commerce wallets: ShopeePay 57%, GoPay 21%, DANA 13%, OVO 6%. — [2C2P](https://2c2p.com/blog/indonesia-payment-methods)
  - Populix: GoPay has the highest brand awareness in 2025. — [Databoks](https://databoks.katadata.co.id/en/technology-telecommunications/statistics/6a4496fc1766a/gopay-e-wallet-with-the-highest-total-awareness-in-indonesia-in-2025)
- QRIS transaction volume rose about 163% year on year (Bank Indonesia, July 2025, via vendor blog). QRIS Tap (NFC) launched in March 2025. — [Tazapay](https://tazapay.com/en-sg/blog/local-payment-methods-in-indonesia-dana). Payment processor Xendit reports QRIS transactions grew 142% and overtook credit cards. — [Kontan](https://keuangan.kontan.co.id/news/transaksi-qris-melalui-xendit-tumbuh-142-lampaui-kartu-kredit)
- Payment-mix claim of e-wallet 39%, bank transfer 23%, cash 14% and credit card 10%. Sourcing is unclear; use with caution. — [Waffo](https://waffo.com/blog/indonesia-payment-market-ecommerce-trends-report)
- Saweria started as a live-stream donation tool and is now a general crowdfunding and tipping platform. Trakteer targets writers, artists, podcasters and others. Fees are about 5% on both (Saweria 6% for OVO). One article (Sept 2024) says Saweria reached 1,000 live streamers in a day and over 1M support-page visits. — [teknologi.id](https://teknologi.id/business/trakteer-dan-saweria-platform-crowdfunding-terlaris-untuk-support-content-creator); [Suara 2024](https://www.suara.com/tekno/2024/06/11/112348/apa-itu-saweria-yang-banyak-digunakan-streamer-lengkap-cara-pemakaiannya); [Detik on Trakteer](https://inet.detik.com/cyberlife/d-7260379/apa-itu-trakteer-yang-bikin-livy-renata-bisa-beli-mobil-baru)

### Inferences
- Mobile-first is right for Indonesia. Mobile speeds are now median 45 Mbps, but coverage outside cities varies, so offline and low-data resilience (PWA caching of SRD content) is still prudent. No direct outage or reliability data was gathered.
- A Saweria or Trakteer link, which accepts QRIS and e-wallets, is the lowest-friction donation path. Any future paid tier would need QRIS or e-wallet support through a local gateway such as Xendit or Midtrans. Card-only checkout would exclude about 98% of adults.

### Gaps
- Mobile vs desktop web-traffic share for Indonesia in 2025–26 (StatCounter not fetched).
- Discord user numbers in Indonesia.
- Total registered users for Saweria and Trakteer.
- Internet reliability outside Java.

## 5. Secondary SEA markets and comparable localisation efforts

### Takeaway
The other SEA markets (the Philippines and Singapore especially) mostly play in English, so language is a weaker barrier there. Their scenes are Facebook, Meetup and Discord-based, with a cross-border indie designer network (#RPGSEA). For localisation precedents, Wizards abandoned Portuguese in 2024 for cost reasons, later handing Brazil to a distributor. That shows official localisation follows sales, and non-official markets depend on fan and third-party tools.

### Cited Findings
- #RPGSEA was created in 2019 by designers from the Philippines, Malaysia and Singapore to find each other. The Session Zero Online convention gathered about 150 designers, fans and merchants from the Philippines, Singapore, Korea and other countries. — [EN World: How online collaboration is opening a world of SEA RPGs](https://www.enworld.org/goto/post?id=8200227); [Kicktraq: Our Shores Kickstarter](https://www.kicktraq.com/articles/redirect/41044)
- Singapore:
  - The hobby gained niche popularity in the 1990s.
  - The "Singapore Tabletop RPGs" Meetup group is one of the most active communities.
  - Studios such as Tinker Tales run D&D 5e, VtM and PF2e in person and online.
  - Adventurers League organisers include a business offering "legitimate" D&D games in Singapore.
  - Sources: [The Urban Wire SG](https://theurbanwire.sg/?p=71989); [Discord.me Singapore servers](https://discord.me/servers/tag/singapore); [The Legitimate Business](https://thelegitimatebusiness.com/); [The Homeground Asia](https://thehomeground.asia/?p=21805)
- Philippines: "Facebook groups consisting of thousands of members" and Tabletop Philippines streams. — [The LaSallian 2020](https://thelasallian.com/2020/01/20/exploring-dd-in-the-philippines/); [The Red Circle PH](https://theredcircle.com.ph/?p=3548)
- Malaysia: thin evidence beyond a Lowyat forum thread linking a Facebook group and a Discord server. — [Lowyat forum](https://forum.lowyat.net/topic/4988011)
- A Republic Asia article frames D&D as a growing Gen Z market in the region (not fetched). — [Republic Asia Media](https://republicasiamedia.com/dungeons-and-dragons-a-growing-gen-z-market-2/)
- Wizards stopped Portuguese translations (announced Feb 2024), citing "rising costs and shifts in global demand". Portuguese-speaking players have often relied on fan translations or piracy. — [ComicBook.com, 24 Feb 2024](https://comicbook.com/gaming/news/dungeons-dragons-discontinues-portuguese-translations). Galápagos was later reported to translate and distribute D&D in Brazil, including the 2024 rules; search snippets give conflicting timing. — [EN World: D&D no longer in Portuguese](https://www.enworld.org/goto/post?id=9273621)
- Official 2024 core rulebooks in French, Italian, German and Spanish are sold in physical markets but not on the D&D Beyond Marketplace. D&D Beyond has no in-app localisation of the 2024 rules. — [D&D Beyond support](https://dndbeyond-support.wizards.com/hc/en-us/articles/46247021698452-Will-the-2024-Core-Rulebooks-be-available-in-other-supported-languages); [D&D Beyond Foreign Language Translations form](https://docs.google.com/forms/d/e/1FAIpQLSdao1V35iQBhI1D9yCNpg6qOAv4dY_UH8Y_4oqtcNWmbFsiZw/viewform)
- Fan tools fill the gap. "DnD Beyond Kit" is a browser extension adding partial Brazilian Portuguese and Spanish translations of the main content, and the Spanish SRD 2024 translation exists as a Foundry VTT module. German fans maintain a full SRD5.1 site (dnddeutsch.de). — [GitHub: dnd-beyond-kit](https://github.com/TaylorHo/dnd-beyond-kit); [Firefox add-on](https://addons.mozilla.org/addon/dnd-beyond-kit/); [Foundry translate-dnd5e-sdr2-es](https://foundryvtt.com/packages/translate-dnd5e-sdr2-es); [dnddeutsch.de](https://www.dnddeutsch.de/srd)

### Inferences
- Common traits of successful localisation precedents:
  - They are built on the openly licensed SRD.
  - They are community-maintained (Foundry modules, extensions, wikis).
  - They piggyback on tools players already use.
  - They fill a gap the publisher left for cost reasons.
- Indonesian is in the same position as Portuguese: no official support and unlikely to get it. That strengthens the case for a fan, community-driven Indonesian tool and suggests a community glossary or crowd-translation model.
- SEA expansion beyond Indonesia should not lean on language. In the Philippines, Singapore and Malaysia, English is widely used, so the differentiators there would be mobile-first UX, free access and local payment support. Malay is close to Indonesian, so a Malay variant could be a cheap extension. This is an estimate and is unverified.

### Gaps
- Thailand and Vietnam: no evidence was gathered on D&D communities, translations or tools.
- Japanese and Korean D&D tool localisation (for example Japanese 5e fan wikis, Korean translations): not researched within the budget.
- Hard evidence for why specific fan localisation tools succeeded (user counts, adoption) was not found. The traits listed above are inferred.
